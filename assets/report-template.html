Method & limits

{{ACCOUNT_TYPE_NOTE}}

The forecast is a trailing eight-week level times a seasonal index from the same weeks a year earlier, damped to {{DAMPING}} and clamped between 0.4 and 2.5. {{SEASONALITY_AVAILABILITY}}

Safety stock is derived from backtested forecast error, not from a cover rule: z times the error standard deviation times the square root of the lead time in weeks. A service level of {{SERVICE_LEVEL}} means roughly one stockout in {{STOCKOUT_ODDS}} replenishment cycles — not that stock is held {{SERVICE_LEVEL}} of days.

Each week of history is a separate pull; there is no daily series in this data and nothing was derived by dividing an aggregate. Stock is consumed against the forecast rather than a flat average, which is why an order-by date can fall earlier than a trailing-rate calculation would suggest.

This is a trailing-level-plus-seasonality forecast and nothing more. It will be wrong on new products, on products whose price changed materially, and on anything driven by a promotion calendar it cannot see. Nothing is ordered here.
